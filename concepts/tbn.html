Tangent space & the TBN basis

GamePBR concepts · how a normal map's 3 stored numbers become a world-space normal, and where the tangent comes from. Companion to step 08.

A tangent-space normal map stores only a normal per texel — three numbers (nx,ny,nz), packed as RGB. The TBN frame is not in the image; it's rebuilt from the mesh at render time (T, N per vertex; B = cross(N,T)). That's why the map is blue: it stores normals that mostly point straight out, (0,0,1) → (0.5,0.5,1).

1 · A tangent-space normal → world

Drag to orbit. The frame T / B / N is glued to the surface. Tilt the stored (tangent-space) normal with the sliders and watch the white world normal swing: n_world = nx·T + ny·B + nz·N. Spin the surface and notice the stored normal and its blue RGB never change — only the world result does. That's the whole reason one little blue image works on any surface.

2 · Where T comes from (UV → tangent)

The tangent is the 3D direction of +U on the surface. Drag the UV vertices on the left; the T and B arrows on the (planar) triangle recompute live from the 2×2 UV solve. Flip a UV across its neighbors (make the area negative) and watch T flip — that's the handedness issue mirrored UVs cause.

Left: UV space (drag the dots). Right: the triangle in 3D (planar), with T/B from the solve.

The formulas, briefly

T = (1/det)·( ΔV2·E1 − ΔV1·E2 ) det = ΔU1·ΔV2 − ΔU2·ΔV1 T ← T − (N·T)N (orthonormalize) B = cross(N, T) n_world = nx·T + ny·B + nz·N = [ T | B | N ] · (nx,ny,nz)

Full derivations are in docs/08. The short story: E = ΔU·T + ΔV·B per edge (because position is affine in UV on a triangle), invert the 2×2 of UV deltas to get T; and n_world is just the decoded normal read back through the basis it was stored in.
